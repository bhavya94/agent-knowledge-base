# Stakeholder Communication

## 1. Know the audience
Before drafting, establish (ask if unknown):
- Who reads it and what they own; what the SLT / their leadership cares about right now.
- Relevant context from Slack threads, email, and docs the user provides: open disagreements,
  prior decisions, who has influence, what's trending.
- What you need from them: a decision, awareness, help, or sign-off.

## 2. Structure
- **Bottom line first** — the ask or the result in one or two sentences.
- **Why it matters** — impact in business terms: $ saved or earned, latency, risk, engineer-hours.
- **Options and recommendation** — 2–3 options with trade-offs and cost; recommend one.
- **Risks and asks** — what could go wrong, what you need, by when.
Match length to channel: Slack ≤ 5 lines plus a link; email ≤ 1 screen; design doc as long as needed.

## 3. Check before sending
- Every number is sourced; assumptions are labeled.
- No jargon the audience won't know; no blame; credit others by name.
- It's clear who should do what next.

Never send anything yourself. Hand the draft to the user.
