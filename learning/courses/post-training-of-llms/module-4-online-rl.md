## What problem?
SFT and DPO are both offline: the training data is fixed in advance, so the model never learns from the mistakes it actually makes at its current weights, and it cannot discover responses better than anything in the dataset. Online RL closes the loop: the current policy generates, a scorer grades the generations, and the policy updates toward what scored well.

## What idea? (lesson 7, Basics of Online RL)
- The loop: sample a batch of prompts, generate responses with the current policy, score each with a reward (a learned reward model in classic RLHF, or a rule/verifier when the answer is checkable), compute advantages, update, repeat. Because the data is generated by the current policy, the method is on-policy.
- GRPO (group relative policy optimization) is the course's algorithm: for each prompt, sample a group of completions, and set each completion's advantage to its reward relative to the group (reward minus group mean, typically normalized by group standard deviation). No separate value network or critic to train; the group is its own baseline. A KL term against the reference policy keeps updates from drifting too far.
- Verifiable rewards are the practical unlock for reasoning tasks: math answers and unit-tested code can be scored exactly, so no reward model needs training and reward hacking has less surface. This is the RLVR pattern behind recent reasoning models.
- Costs and risks: generation dominates compute; training is less stable than SFT/DPO; a poorly specified reward gets gamed (reward hacking); entropy collapse can narrow exploration if the KL and sampling settings are off.

## What practice? (lesson 8, Online RL in Practice; notebook Lesson_7.ipynb)
- Task: GSM8K math word problems. The reward is a plain Python function: regex-extract the content of \boxed{...} from the completion, return 1.0 if it equals the ground truth, else 0.0. No learned scorer anywhere.
- Baseline first: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct evaluated on a small slice (5 examples) before any training, predictions stored against ground truths.
- Training with TRL's GRPO trainer: GRPOConfig with num_generations 4 per prompt (the notebook notes this can go to 64 or 128 in real runs), learning rate 5e-6, per-device batch 1 with gradient accumulation 8, 1 epoch, CPU-feasible.
- Close: load the fully trained Qwen checkpoint and compare results on the same eval slice.

## Takeaways
- Eval before training, on a tiny slice, with the same reward you will train against. If the reward function is wrong, everything downstream is wasted compute.
- GRPO's group baseline is why this is teachable in one notebook: policy, reference, reward function; that is the whole cast.
- The recipe generalizes: any task where I can write a checker (exact match, tests, schema validation) is a candidate for this stage.
